# Furniture Pros — Landing Page Design Direction

## Objective

Create a single high-end landing page for **Furniture Pros**, a furniture restoration, refinishing, repair, and redesign business serving Roseville, Sacramento, and surrounding areas.

This is **not a full website**.

The page has one primary objective:

> Make visitors trust the craftsmanship, appreciate the transformation work, and send photos of their furniture to request a quote.

The page should feel specifically art-directed for Furniture Pros.

---

# Design Direction

The visual direction is:

**Refined interiors magazine + master furniture workshop + understated heritage luxury**

The landing page should feel:

- Warm
- Premium
- Established
- Tactile
- Craftsmanship-led
- Residential
- Refined
- Trustworthy
- Timeless

It should communicate skilled furniture craftsmanship rather than generic home services.

---

# Do Not Make It Look Like

Never make Furniture Pros resemble:

- A SaaS startup
- A technology company
- A generic contractor
- A handyman website
- A furniture retailer
- An interior-design agency
- A generic AI-generated landing page
- A template with the company name swapped in

Avoid the recognizable Claude/AI landing-page aesthetic.

---

# Core Visual Principle

## Craftsmanship Before Decoration

The page should look expensive because of:

- excellent typography
- strong furniture photography
- composition
- whitespace
- proportion
- natural colors
- subtle details

Not because of:

- gradients
- effects
- excessive cards
- animations
- shadows
- decorative UI

Photography and typography should do most of the visual work.

---

Use warm, furniture-inspired colors.

### Warm Ivory

Main page background.

### Parchment
`#E4D9C6`

Secondary section background.

### Furniture Pros Olive
`#46553A`

Primary brand color.

### Deep Olive
`#303B2B`

Dark sections and strong contrast.

### Walnut
`#553A27`

Small warm material accents.

### Dark Walnut
`#34251C`

Alternative dark background/text.

### Aged Brass
`#A38655`

Use sparingly for refined accents.

### Charcoal Brown
`#29251F`

---

# Typography

Use a refined serif paired with a clean sans-serif.

## Headings

Use a sophisticated serif such as:

- Cormorant Garamond
- Libre Baskerville
- Lora
- EB Garamond
- DM Serif Display

The serif should feel connected to:

- furniture
- craftsmanship
- heritage
- quality interiors

Avoid overly decorative or fashionable fonts.

## Body

Use:

- Inter
- Source Sans 3
- Manrope
- Geist

Use serif typography for major headings and important statements.

Use sans-serif for:

- body copy
- navigation
- buttons
- labels
- metadata

Avoid excessive bold typography.

---

# Layout

The landing page should feel editorial rather than component-driven.

Prefer:

- asymmetric layouts
- strong left alignment
- large photography
- offset text
- generous margins
- varied image proportions
- thin divider lines
- restrained use of containers

Do not center every section.

Do not put everything inside cards.

Do not repeat exactly the same two-column structure throughout the page.

Maximum content width:

`1200–1400px`

---

# Corners

Keep corners restrained.

Typical radius:

`0–6px`

Do not use giant rounded containers.

Avoid:

- 20px+ radius cards
- pill-shaped sections
- rounded floating CTA boxes
- bubbly UI

---

# Shadows

Use little to no shadow.

Prefer:

- whitespace
- borders
- tonal contrast
- photography
- composition

over floating components.

---

# Photography

Photography is the most important visual asset after typography.

Prioritize:

### Transformation
- Before and after furniture
- Worn vs restored surfaces
- Old paint vs refinished wood
- Damaged vs repaired pieces
- Outdated vs redesigned furniture

### Finished Work
- Dressers
- Tables
- Chairs
- Cabinets
- Sideboards
- Desks
- Heirloom furniture
- Antique furniture
- Solid wood pieces

### Craftsmanship Details
- Wood grain
- Hand sanding
- Refinishing
- Staining
- Paint removal
- Joinery
- Hardware
- Surface repair
- Hands doing actual restoration work

Avoid generic contractor stock photography.

Avoid fake-looking AI furniture imagery.

---

# Landing Page Structure

Keep the page focused.

Do not add sections simply to make the landing page longer.

Use approximately **6 primary sections**.

---

## 1. Hero

The hero should immediately communicate transformation and craftsmanship.

Preferred layout:

### Left
Small location/service label.

Example:

`FURNITURE RESTORATION · ROSEVILLE & SACRAMENTO`

Large serif headline:

# Love Your Furniture Again.

Supporting text:

Furniture Pros restores, refinishes, repairs, and redesigns furniture worth keeping.

Primary CTA:

**Send Us a Photo**

Secondary text link:

**View Transformations**

### Right

Use exceptional restored furniture imagery or a strong before/after composition.

Avoid a generic centered hero.

Do not use two giant buttons.

Do not use a gradient background.

---

# 2. Transformation Proof

Show impressive before/after work immediately after the hero.

Before/after should be a major visual feature, not a small card.

Use:

**BEFORE**

and

**AFTER**

labels clearly.

Include minimal supporting information explaining what was changed.

The transformation itself should provide the proof.

---

# 3. What We Do

Present services as a refined editorial list rather than cards.

Example:

`01 — Scratch & Dent Repair`

`02 — Restore & Refinish`

`03 — Stain & Color Changes`

`04 — Old Paint Removal`

`05 — Custom Furniture Redesign`

`06 — Structural Repairs`

Use thin rules between services.

A photograph or detail image may accompany the list.

Do not put six icons inside six rounded cards.

---

# 4. Craftsmanship / Featured Work

Show why Furniture Pros is different from ordinary furniture repair.

Use one strong project or craftsmanship composition.

Possible structure:

Large furniture image on one side.

Editorial text on the other.

Explain briefly:

- what condition the piece was in
- what needed to change
- what restoration work was performed
- the finished result

Keep the copy concise.

Let the furniture remain visually dominant.

---

# 5. Trust + Testimonials

Use one or several strong customer testimonials.

Testimonials should feel editorial rather than app-like.

Example:

Large serif quotation.

Then:

Customer name  
Furniture/project type  
Roseville or Sacramento

Include genuine trust signals when available.

Do not invent:

- reviews
- awards
- project counts
- years in business
- certifications

---

# 6. Final Conversion Section

End with a strong visual CTA.

Suggested direction:

## Have a Piece Worth Saving?

Send us a few photos and tell us what you'd like restored, repaired, refinished, or changed.

**Send Us a Photo →**

Reinforce:

`Serving Roseville, Sacramento & surrounding areas.`

This should be the strongest conversion moment after the hero.

---

# Primary CTA

The key action throughout the landing page is:

## Send Us a Photo

This is more appropriate than generic CTA language.

Use variations such as:

- Send Us a Photo
- Show Us Your Furniture
- Request a Furniture Quote

Avoid:

- Get Started
- Start Now
- Unlock
- Discover More
- Learn More

when a specific action is available.

---

# Copy Style

Copy should sound:

- experienced
- knowledgeable
- warm
- straightforward
- specific

Avoid excessive luxury language.

Avoid phrases such as:

- unparalleled craftsmanship
- exceptional solutions
- elevate your space
- transform your lifestyle
- where quality meets excellence
- your vision, our passion
- bringing dreams to life

Use furniture-specific language instead.

---

# Anti-Claude / Anti-AI Design Rules

These rules are mandatory.

Do NOT default to:

- blue or purple gradients
- gradient text
- glowing effects
- glassmorphism
- huge rounded rectangles
- floating cards
- excessive pill elements
- bento grids
- three identical cards in a row
- six identical feature cards
- icon + heading + paragraph repeated everywhere
- Lucide icons as decoration
- abstract blobs
- floating circles
- random geometric shapes
- decorative waves
- excessive shadows
- giant centered headlines
- centered content everywhere
- fake statistics
- logo strips without reason
- generic badges
- excessive animation
- oversized CTA panels
- dashboard-like UI
- generic stock imagery

---

# Forbidden Landing Page Pattern

Do not create:

**Navbar**

↓

**Centered giant headline**

↓

**Two buttons**

↓

**Large rounded image**

↓

**Three feature cards**

↓

**Three more cards**

↓

**Statistics**

↓

**Testimonial cards**

↓

**Large rounded CTA**

↓

**Footer**

This is exactly the generic AI-generated landing page appearance this design system exists to prevent.

---

# Visual Variety

The page should have visual rhythm.

Alternate between:

- full-width photography
- asymmetric image/text layouts
- narrow editorial copy
- before/after comparisons
- service indexes
- dark olive sections
- warm ivory sections

Do not make each section structurally identical.

---

# Motion

Keep motion subtle.

Allowed:

- gentle image reveals
- restrained fade/slide
- subtle image zoom on hover
- elegant text-link underline
- before/after interaction

Avoid:

- bouncing
- spinning
- dramatic parallax
- constant animations
- glowing effects
- cursor gimmicks

The experience should feel calm and deliberate.

---

# Mobile

Mobile must still feel premium.

Do not merely stack every desktop block mechanically.

Prioritize:

1. Headline
2. Furniture imagery
3. Before/after transformation
4. Services
5. Trust
6. Send-a-photo CTA

Keep typography readable.

Avoid excessive vertical whitespace.

Keep the photo/quote CTA obvious.

---

# Final Design Check

Before considering the landing page finished, review it specifically for generic AI design patterns.

Ask:

### Does this look like Claude's default landing page?

If yes, redesign it.

### Does it immediately look like a furniture restoration business?

If no, redesign it.

### Is the furniture itself one of the strongest visual elements?

If no, redesign it.

### Does the page feel warm, tactile, established, and craftsmanship-led?

If no, redesign it.

### Is the design relying on composition and photography rather than cards and decoration?

If no, simplify it.

### Would this feel appropriate for someone considering restoration of an expensive, antique, heirloom, or meaningful piece of furniture?

If no, improve the art direction.

---

# Final Standard

Furniture Pros should feel like a **contemporary master furniture-restoration atelier**.

The page should combine:

**traditional craftsmanship + beautiful furniture + warm residential sophistication + modern editorial web design**

It should not look like a template made for a contractor.

It should look like Furniture Pros commissioned a skilled designer who understands furniture, materials, restoration, and craftsmanship.
